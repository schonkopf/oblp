Kueishan Island is a volcanic island off northeastern Taiwan. A shallow hydrothermal system occurs on its eastern side, while subtropical reef and rocky habitats surround other parts of the island. From August 2020 to October 2022, researchers repeatedly surveyed six sites at approximately 1–3 month intervals. The sites form a spatial gradient from the western turtle-tail reef at T, through northern and southern habitats, to the eastern hydrothermal and CO2 vent environments.

VN and VF were distinct vent-associated sampling sites: VF lay approximately 30 m south of VN. The coordinates for VF reflect this relative location rather than an original GPS measurement; coordinates for N, S, and C are likewise approximate cartographic estimates. T and VN are the verified locations that later connect this finite survey to the west and east long-term monitoring stations, respectively.

At each site, seawater physicochemical conditions and underwater soundscapes were measured as complementary observations. Variables included temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, sulfide, total alkalinity, and dissolved inorganic carbon. Measurements used a Multi 3620 IDS multiparameter meter with SenTix 940 pH and FDO 925 optical dissolved-oxygen sensors, and an AS-C3 dissolved inorganic carbon analyzer.

Acoustic recordings used a SoundTrap ST300 STD with a reported sensitivity of −175 dB re 1 V/µPa and a 20 Hz–60 kHz (±3 dB) working bandwidth. Standardized samples supported comparison of acoustic energy in the 20–200 Hz, 200 Hz–2 kHz, and 2–20 kHz bands alongside the physicochemical observations. This design enabled spatial and temporal comparison of conditions associated with changing hydrothermal activity.
