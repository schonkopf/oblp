The two-year observations identified a sequence of an initial active hydrothermal period, a silent or reduced-venting period, and a second active period. Temporal changes at the VN near-vent site included shifts in pH, dissolved inorganic carbon, sulfide, pCO2, alkalinity, and carbonate chemistry consistent with these changing hydrothermal states. Renewed activity after the quiet period occurred in temporal association with clusters of nearby shallow earthquakes, sulfurous smoke events, and renewed physicochemical changes at VN. This association suggests that seismic activity may influence venting through subsurface fluid dynamics, but does not by itself demonstrate that the earthquakes caused reactivation.

Repeated observations at T, N, S, VN, VF, and C showed how those transitions extended across the surrounding habitats. VN and VF were separate physical sampling locations, with VF approximately 30 m south of VN. During active venting, eastern vent-associated conditions differed more strongly from the western T reef reference; during reduced venting, conditions across some habitats became more similar; and after renewed activity, spatial differentiation increased again. Responses varied among habitats rather than occurring identically everywhere. Principal component analysis characterized major physicochemical gradients, PERMANOVA identified contributions from site and temporal period, and UMAP visualized changing similarities among habitats across hydrothermal states.

Soundscapes also varied among sites and periods. T and VN represented contrasting acoustic environments: stronger high-frequency biological acoustic energy at T was consistent with substantial reef-associated activity, including snapping shrimp, whereas increased low-frequency energy near the vent environment was associated with active venting and may partly reflect bubbling and other geophysical sound. Not every low-frequency sound can be assigned to hydrothermal activity. During reduced venting, the acoustic conditions of some sites became more similar, followed by renewed spatial differentiation as vent activity returned.

Together, the results show that Kueishan Island is not a static vent-versus-reef system. Hydrothermal variability reshapes environmental gradients through time, while the comparatively stable western T reef provides an ecological and acoustic reference for interpreting change in the eastern vent-associated habitats. Soundscape observations complement physicochemical measurements by integrating geophysical processes, biological activity, and environmental variability; acoustic energy alone, however, does not directly measure biodiversity or establish the mechanism driving hydrothermal change.
