Approximately 2.5 years of acoustic data revealed a complex environment containing biological, environmental, and anthropogenic sources. The analysis used source-specific periodicity to factorize long-duration time–frequency representations, separating characteristic spectral components associated with cetaceans, soniferous fishes, and abiotic sources with minimal supervision.

The separated sound sources followed distinct temporal patterns. Biological acoustic activity varied over diel and seasonal scales, and the analysis identified temporal interactions between cetacean and soniferous-fish acoustic activity. These patterns describe sound-source dynamics and temporal co-occurrence; they do not establish organism abundance, identify every vocalizing species, or demonstrate a causal ecological interaction.

The results show how long-term passive acoustics can provide high-temporal-resolution information about offshore ecosystem dynamics. Soundscape observations remain one line of ecological evidence rather than a complete measure of ecosystem health.
