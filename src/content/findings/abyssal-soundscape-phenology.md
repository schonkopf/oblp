The year-long record showed clear diel and seasonal variability. Evening biological choruses were a major contributor to these temporal patterns, and marine mammal acoustic activity also varied through time. The choruses may originate from mid-water fishes rather than organisms on the abyssal seafloor.

Because these sounds propagate into the aphotic deep sea, they may provide temporal environmental information and could act as a potential cue for organisms living without direct sunlight. This ecological interpretation has not been demonstrated behaviorally, but it identifies soundscape monitoring as a useful approach for investigating deep-sea phenology and chronobiology.
