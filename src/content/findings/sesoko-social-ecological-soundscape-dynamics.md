At the 20 m Sesoko reef, biological choruses showed strong diel and seasonal patterns. Nighttime fish choruses were especially evident during warmer months, and their intensity also varied with tidal and lunar phases. Daytime vessel noise reflected human activity around the island, including regular ferry traffic, and was particularly evident during the 2019 Golden Week holiday. Wind and wave noise recorded environmental forcing, increasing especially during typhoon and northeastern monsoon seasons.

The comparison between Golden Week in 2019 and the 2020 holiday during Japan's first nationwide COVID-19 state of emergency showed reduced ferry and fishing-vessel noise during the 2020 anthropause. New fish sounds were also detected in the nighttime chorus during this period, although the observations did not establish whether their appearance was directly caused by reduced vessel noise.

The analysis combined long-term spectrograms, source separation, and soundscape_IR to distinguish temporal dynamics associated with biological choruses, anthropogenic noise, and environmental noise. Together, these acoustic indicators illustrate how a single passive monitoring system can provide complementary observations of ecological activity, human activity, and environmental disturbance, supporting study of coral reefs as social-ecological systems without replacing ecological surveys or treating soundscape activity alone as a measure of biodiversity or reef health.
